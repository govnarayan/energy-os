How solar proposals mislead

In 2026, state attorneys general took action against solar sellers over these claims. All are allegations.

Savings that assume rate hikes

A guessed yearly rise in utility prices, presented as savings you get today.

Michigan Attorney General, July 2026

Fees buried in the price

Lender fees folded into the system price, so the loan looks cheaper than it is. Michigan cites an average of 27%.

Michigan, New York and Kansas Attorneys General, 2026

"You'll never pay a power bill"

Promises of no bill, or of free systems and roofs funded by programs that did not exist.

Kansas and New York Attorneys General, 2026

Tax credits that were not there

Wrong claims about who qualifies. The federal homeowner credit ended for systems placed in service after Dec 31, 2025.

Michigan and Texas Attorneys General; IRS

What they ask

The basics needed to put panels on your roof.

Your home

Address, ownership, roof material and age, shade, HOA and permit rules.

Your electrical setup

Panel rating and age, sub-panels, any existing solar or battery.

Your usage

A recent bill or a year of data, and your goal: backup power, budget, cash or loan.

What they don't ask

The questions that decide whether solar pays off. Most go unasked, or get a one-sided answer.

Your home

  • HVAC, today and tomorrowThe system you have and the one you will install set how much power you use.
  • Insulation and ductingEfficiency work shrinks your load before a single panel goes up.
  • Temperature preferencesYour heating and cooling ranges decide when and how much power you draw.

Your utility

  • Electric rate plansStandard, electrification or solar. Each changes what a kWh costs and what solar earns.
  • Gas ratesThe price of the fuel your electricity would replace.
  • Rate inflation and deflationIncluding local minimums and caps. Past increases do not guarantee future ones.

Your equipment

  • TechnologyMicroinverters or string inverters, matched to your roof, not the installer's catalog.
  • BrandsInstaller margin kept apart from reviews and customer service.
  • Panel reinstallA new roof means removing and refitting every panel. Get it priced upfront.

Your real return

  • BreakevenBuilt on forecast grid resale and your battery capacity, not a flat rate.
  • The cost of the summer creditWhat you spend to qualify for the resale credit you were pitched.
  • Annual maintenanceThe ongoing cost your payback math should carry.

How to read any solar proposal

Nine questions that separate a good system from a good pitch.

QuestionProposals usually showCheck instead
1SizingA yearly offset percentage, sometimes 120% or more.Production against use, month by month. Size to the worst month: extra panels add cost, not savings.
2Export valueLow resale rates, made up for by a summer utility credit.Nobody pitches exports at the retail rate. Only power used at home earns full price. Test the summer credit like the battery claim below: what it pays, on which days, and what it costs to qualify.
3BatteryPeak-sale income of $150 to $300 a month.Whether the grid's peak credit beats the retail price you avoid. In the owner's own PG&E data that happened only a few days a year. Size the battery to shift your own evening use.
4Rate inflationSavings that grow with yearly utility increases.Past increases do not predict future ones. Moving to an electrification rate plan can save more on your bill than solar does, so make that plan the baseline for ROI.
5InvertersMicroinverters, always.They pay off with heavy shade or long, low sun. Otherwise string inverters deliver the same energy for less.
6PanelsWatts and brand.Price per usable kWh after losses, plus degradation and warranty terms.
7EV"Drive for free."Spare solar by month. Outside summer afternoons there is little, and sizing up for a car costs more than gas or fast charging.
8FinancingA low monthly payment.Total cost over the term, with every lender fee. Leases and PPAs may still pass through the owner's federal credit, with deadlines. Know which you are signing.
9Future loadToday's bill, plus room for what comes next.Installers do ask about heat pumps and EVs, and utilities allow some oversizing to cover them. Use that allowance for loads you will add, and nothing beyond it.
